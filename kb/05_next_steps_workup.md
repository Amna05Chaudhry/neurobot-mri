# Next steps after an MRI suggests a glioma

An imaging finding that looks like a glioma is not a diagnosis. Typical next steps, decided by the treating doctors, include:

1. Review by a neuroradiologist of the full MRI study (all sequences, all slices), comparison with any earlier scans, and often extra sequences such as contrast-enhanced T1, perfusion or spectroscopy.
2. Referral to a neurosurgeon and a multidisciplinary neuro-oncology team (tumour board).
3. Tissue diagnosis by biopsy or surgical resection. The pathologist determines tumour type and grade, and molecular tests (IDH mutation, 1p/19q codeletion, ATRX, TP53, CDKN2A/B, MGMT promoter methylation) refine the diagnosis under the WHO 2021 classification.
4. Seizure management with anti-seizure medication if seizures have occurred.
5. Baseline assessments (neurological examination, sometimes neuropsychological testing) and advice on driving after seizures according to local rules.

Helpful questions for a patient to ask: What type and grade of tumour is it likely to be, and how will that be confirmed? Is surgery possible, and how much can be safely removed? Which molecular tests will be done? Which symptoms should prompt urgent attention? How often will follow-up MRI be done? Are clinical trials available?

Symptoms that need immediate medical care include a first seizure, sudden severe headache, new weakness, loss of speech or vision, confusion, or reduced consciousness.
