# Treatment overview for lower-grade glioma

Treatment is individualised by a multidisciplinary team based on tumour type, grade, molecular markers, location, symptoms, age and patient preference.

Surgery: maximal safe resection is usually the first step, both to obtain tissue for diagnosis and because a more complete resection is associated with longer survival. When the tumour lies in eloquent brain, a biopsy or partial resection may be chosen instead.

Radiation therapy and chemotherapy: for higher-risk lower-grade gliomas (for example older age, incomplete resection, or grade 3), radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy (PCV: procarbazine, lomustine, vincristine; or temozolomide) is a standard option and improved survival in clinical trials.

Targeted therapy: in August 2024 the US FDA approved vorasidenib, an oral IDH1/IDH2 inhibitor, for adults and children aged 12 and older with grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma with a susceptible IDH1 or IDH2 mutation, following surgery. In the INDIGO trial it prolonged progression-free survival compared with placebo.

Active surveillance: some patients with low-risk grade 2 tumours after surgery are followed with regular MRI scans, delaying radiation and chemotherapy to avoid their long-term side effects.

Follow-up: MRI is repeated at regular intervals (often every 3 to 6 months at first) to watch for growth or new contrast enhancement, which may signal progression.
