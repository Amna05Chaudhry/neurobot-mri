# Lower-grade glioma (LGG): overview

Gliomas are primary brain tumours that arise from glial (supporting) cells. "Lower-grade glioma" usually refers to diffusely infiltrating gliomas of WHO CNS grade 2 and 3. They grow more slowly than glioblastoma (grade 4) but infiltrate the surrounding brain, so surgery alone rarely cures them.

Since the 2021 WHO classification (CNS5), adult diffuse gliomas are defined by molecular markers as well as histology. The two main lower-grade types are astrocytoma, IDH-mutant (grades 2 to 4) and oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted (grades 2 to 3). An IDH-wildtype diffuse astrocytic tumour with certain molecular features (TERT promoter mutation, EGFR amplification, or chromosome +7/-10) is classified as glioblastoma even if it looks lower-grade under the microscope.

IDH mutation and 1p/19q codeletion matter clinically: they carry prognostic information and affect treatment choice. Oligodendrogliomas generally have the most favourable outlook among adult diffuse gliomas.

Lower-grade gliomas most often affect younger adults (roughly 20 to 50 years). They frequently arise in the frontal and temporal lobes. The most common first symptom is a seizure; headaches, personality change or focal neurological deficits can also occur depending on location.
