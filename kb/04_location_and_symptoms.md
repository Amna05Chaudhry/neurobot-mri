# Tumour location and what it can mean

Each brain hemisphere controls the opposite side of the body: a lesion in the left hemisphere tends to affect the right side, and vice versa. In most right-handed people (and many left-handed people) language is mainly in the left hemisphere.

Frontal lobe: movement (motor cortex), planning, judgement, behaviour and personality; in the dominant hemisphere, speech production (Broca's area). Frontal tumours may cause weakness, personality change or seizures.

Temporal lobe: hearing, memory, and in the dominant hemisphere language comprehension (Wernicke's area). Temporal lobe tumours commonly cause seizures, including focal seizures with altered awareness or unusual sensations.

Parietal lobe: sensation, spatial awareness and orientation. Lesions may cause numbness, neglect or difficulty with spatial tasks.

Occipital lobe: vision. Lesions may cause loss of the visual field on the opposite side.

Tumours near "eloquent" cortex (motor, language, vision areas) need careful surgical planning, often with functional MRI, tractography, or awake surgery with brain mapping.

NeuroBot reports location only as hemisphere and image quadrant on a single axial slice. It does not identify lobes; mapping a lesion to anatomy requires a radiologist reviewing the full 3-D study.
